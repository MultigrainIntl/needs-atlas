## Poverty, income, and the case for concentrated need
The communities Zufall Health serves within Somerset County, New Jersey include areas of concentrated poverty and constrained access to care. Somerset County's median household income is $140,374, above the statewide median of $103,556, and 5.8% of residents live in poverty, below the New Jersey rate of 9.2%.[1] Poverty alone understates the strain: 29.8% of Somerset County households fall below the ALICE threshold — unable to afford basic necessities despite, in most cases, being employed (New Jersey: 39.4%).[2] County averages, however, flatten the neighborhoods where need concentrates — which a census-tract view brings into focus.

## Where the need concentrates: a census-tract view
Somerset County contains 74 census tracts. Measured against the seven-county service-area average, 19 of those tracts carry an above-average uninsured rate and 13 carry an above-average share of residents below 200% of the federal poverty level.[3] 12% of the county's residents — roughly 42,329 people — live in tracts where at least one in four residents falls below 200% of the federal poverty level. The sharpest need sits in North Plainfield (Census Tract 517, GEOID 34035051700): there, 27% of residents are uninsured and 48% live below 200% of the federal poverty level, and 18% of households are limited-English-speaking — a concentration that county-level averages hide entirely. Across the seven counties Zufall serves, Somerset carries the fourth-highest composite need. This is precisely the sub-county geography a grant application must document, and it is where Zufall's sites and outreach can be targeted for greatest effect.

## Barriers to care: insurance and providers
Access to coverage is a clear barrier. 6.4% of Somerset County residents under age 65 are uninsured, below the statewide rate of 9.1%.[1] Provider supply reinforces the gap: Somerset County has roughly one primary-care physician per 907 residents, one dentist per 1017, and one mental-health provider per 307 — against statewide figures of 1279, 1157, and 320 respectively.[4]

## Chronic disease and community health burden
Model-based CDC estimates put adult diabetes in Somerset County at 10.1% (New Jersey: 10.9%), obesity at 26.3% (NJ: 28.9%), and high blood pressure at 30.6% (NJ: 32.9%).[5] Somerset exceeds the statewide rate on 1 of the eleven tracked chronic conditions, including cancer (non-skin). 13% of adults report fair or poor overall health (NJ: 17.1%) and 12.6% report frequent mental distress (NJ: 14.7%). On health-related social needs, 5.5% of adults report a lack of reliable transportation and 8.7% report housing insecurity (NJ: 7.4% and 12.3%). These conditions define the day-to-day clinical demand a community health center must meet — and, paired with the coverage and language barriers documented above, the case for sustained primary, dental, and behavioral-health capacity. Birth outcomes reflect the same pressures: 7% of live births in Somerset County are low birthweight (New Jersey: 8%).[4]

## Food insecurity
An estimated 8.8% of Somerset County residents — about 30,630 people — live in food-insecure households, and 9.5% of the county's children face food insecurity.[6] Food insecurity compounds the barriers documented here: it tracks closely with low income and uninsurance and drives demand for the wraparound support Zufall's sites provide.

## Demographics and language access
Somerset County is home to 356,486 residents. 18.9% identify as Hispanic or Latino and 11.3% as Black or African American, compared with 24.0% and 15.8% statewide.[1] Language access is a substantial need: 34.7% of residents age 5 and over speak a language other than English at home, above the New Jersey figure of 33.1%.[1] Adults age 65 and over make up 18.9% of the population (New Jersey: 18.5%).[1]
